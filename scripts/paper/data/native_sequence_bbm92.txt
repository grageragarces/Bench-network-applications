BBM92 written directly on SeQUeNCe 1.0.0, against qnetbench's engine at the same link fidelity.
Two routers 1 km apart, 1 ms classical delay, decoherence disabled, 32 seeds per row.
QBER = rounds breaking the Bell-pair correlation / rounds kept after sifting. The closed form for a Werner
pair at fidelity F is 2 (1 - F) / 3.

     F   kept  broken  native SeQUeNCe   qnetbench  closed form
  0.99    442       0           0.0000      0.0050       0.0067
  0.95    442       0           0.0000      0.0383       0.0333
  0.85    442       0           0.0000      0.1060       0.1000
  0.70    442       0           0.0000      0.2076       0.2000
  0.60    442       0           0.0000      0.2642       0.2667

The native protocol, including the SeQUeNCe reservation that generates the pairs, takes 54 lines
of code. In qnetbench the same experiment is one TenantSpec, and it runs on all three supplies.

SeQUeNCe's pairs carry no link noise, so a protocol written on it measures a perfect pair at every
fidelity. Reproducing what qnetbench reports would mean adding the noise by hand.
